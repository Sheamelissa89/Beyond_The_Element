Classical elements belong to historical theories of nature. Modern chemical elements are defined by their number of protons.

Fire

Fire represented heat and transformation in classical thought.

A modern connection

Ordinary flames involve chemical reactions that release energy as heat and light. Fire is not a chemical element.

Air

Air was understood as a basic substance associated with breath and the atmosphere.

A modern connection

Earth's air is a mixture, mainly nitrogen and oxygen, with argon, carbon dioxide, and other gases.

Earth

Earth represented solidity and the substance of land, stones, and the physical world.

A modern connection

Rocks and soils contain many minerals and compounds. They are not made from a single chemical element.

Water

Water represented fluidity and was treated as a fundamental substance in classical thought.

A modern connection

Water is the compound H₂O. Each molecule contains two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom.

Aether

In Aristotle's account, aether was the substance associated with the heavens and celestial bodies.

A modern connection

Modern astronomy explains stars and planets through matter, radiation, and physical laws. Aether is not an element in the periodic table.

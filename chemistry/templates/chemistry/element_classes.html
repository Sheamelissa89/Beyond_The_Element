Patterns within the periodic table

Element Classifications

Metals, nonmetals, and metalloids help us describe patterns in element properties and behavior.

Explore the History of Classification →

These are introductory examples. Properties vary between elements, and the boundary of the metalloid group can differ between references.

Metals

Metals generally conduct heat and electricity well. Many are shiny, malleable, and ductile.

Element examples

  • Iron — Fe
  • Copper — Cu
  • Aluminum — Al
  • Gold — Au
  • Mercury — Hg

Metals occupy most of the left and center of the periodic table. Mercury is liquid at room temperature.

Nonmetals

Nonmetals have varied properties. Many conduct electricity poorly, and solid nonmetals are often brittle.

Element examples

  • Hydrogen — H
  • Carbon — C
  • Nitrogen — N
  • Oxygen — O
  • Sulfur — S
  • Bromine — Br

Most are on the right of the periodic table, with hydrogen on the left. They include solids, liquids, and gases.

Metalloids

Metalloids show a mixture of metallic and nonmetallic properties. Some are useful semiconductor materials.

Commonly included elements

  • Boron — B
  • Silicon — Si
  • Germanium — Ge
  • Arsenic — As
  • Antimony — Sb
  • Tellurium — Te

These elements lie near the staircase boundary between metals and nonmetals.

A changing understanding of matter

History of Chemistry

People have explored matter through observation, practical craft, philosophical ideas, and experimental science. Follow some of the connections between those approaches.

The Classical Elements

In ancient Greek thought, earth, water, air, and fire offered a way to explain the variety and transformation of matter. Aristotle also described aether in connection with the heavens.

These ideas influenced later natural philosophy, although they differ from the experimental definitions used in chemistry today.

Understanding States of Matter

Everyday experiences such as melting ice, boiling water, and condensing steam raised questions about how matter changes.

Experimental study and particle theories helped explain these changes through particle motion, interactions, temperature, and pressure.

Explore States of Matter →

Grouping the Elements

People worked with metals long before modern atomic theory. As chemical knowledge grew, comparisons of conductivity, appearance, and reactions helped reveal recurring properties.

Categories such as metals, nonmetals, and metalloids provide useful descriptions, while the periodic table reveals more detailed patterns.

Explore Element Classifications →

The Periodic Table

Early arrangements compared element properties and atomic weights. The modern periodic table orders elements by atomic number and displays recurring patterns in their behavior.

Its structure connects the identity of an element with the arrangement of electrons in its atoms.

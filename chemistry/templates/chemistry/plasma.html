The Science of Charged Matter

Plasma Laboratory

From the heart of stars to the glow of neon, discover the extraordinary fourth familiar state of matter.

Plasma is a gas-like state of matter containing free electrons and ions, often alongside neutral particles.

Because charged particles respond collectively to electromagnetic fields, plasma behaves differently from ordinary neutral gas.

How Does Plasma Form?

01 · Neutral Gas

Atoms and molecules move freely. Most particles have no net electric charge.

02 · Energy Input

Collisions with energetic electrons or other processes can remove electrons from atoms and molecules.

03 · Ionization

Some particles become positively charged ions while free electrons move through the surrounding material.

04 · Plasma Behavior

Charged particles interact with electric and magnetic fields, producing collective behavior and sometimes visible light.

The Four Familiar States of Matter

Matter behaves differently depending on its conditions and the interactions between its particles.

🧊

Solid

Particles remain close together, generally vibrating around relatively fixed positions.

💧

Liquid

Particles remain close together but can move past one another.

💨

Gas

Particles move freely and spread throughout their container.

⚡

Plasma

A gas-like collection of charged and often neutral particles capable of collective electromagnetic behavior.

Plasma Throughout Our Universe

Plasma appears in extraordinary natural environments and important technologies.

☀️

Stellar Plasma

Stars contain enormous quantities of ionized matter. Their magnetic fields help shape dramatic activity.

🌌

Atmospheric Plasma

Lightning creates ionized channels. Auroras occur when energetic particles excite atmospheric gases.

💜

Noble-Gas Plasma

Electrical discharges in gases such as neon and argon can create beautiful characteristic light.

🚀

Technological Plasma

Plasma is used in manufacturing, lighting, materials processing, and certain spacecraft thrusters.
